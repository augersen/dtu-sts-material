   Statement: We observe that the Danish power system is shifting from centralized power plants to dispersed generation, including local CHP units and large offshore wind farms. This transition reduces reliance on centralized plants for voltage and frequency control, presenting challenges for maintaining grid stability. Currently, centralized plants are essential for stabilizing the grid during faults, as dispersed generation frequently disconnects under such conditions.

To address these challenges, we ensure that offshore wind farms comply with grid requirements, including ride-through capabilities. Fixed-speed turbines utilize blade-angle control to minimize power during faults, while variable-speed turbines rely on crowbar protection and pitch control. These technologies play a critical role in stabilizing turbines during faults but still depend on centralized plants to re-establish grid stability. Drawing from these experiences, we aim to inform the design and operation of a future power system with a growing share of wind power.

9. ID: 1894
   Year: 2007
   Cluster: (None)
   Actor: PlanEnergi
   Statement: We believe the Samsø Energy Island project must be regarded as a near-complete success. Our primary objective—achieving 100% self-sufficiency with renewable energy using local resources—has been accomplished. This achievement includes the total elimination of CO₂ emissions and other air pollutants. A key factor behind this success is the active mobilization and support of the local community, who embraced and adopted the project.

However, not all conservation objectives have been met. In particular, goals related to heating and electricity efficiency were not fully achieved, and transportation remains largely unchanged, still dependent on non-renewable energy sources.

This project was an enormous undertaking for a small community, yet they completed it on schedule. But could the same be done on a national scale? There are significant challenges to consider. National energy consumption per capita is 25% higher than that of Samsø. Additionally, Denmark has only one-third of the biomass resources per capita compared to Samsø, and the national wind resource potential per person is also lower.

If Samsø’s contribution is viewed as part of Denmark's national renewable energy (RE) supply, two scenarios emerge:

If based on Samsø’s land and resource capacity, RE production would need to increase fourfold.
If based on Samsø’s population proportion, RE production must increase by 25%.
In either case, a significant increase in renewable energy production would be required. These observations are not intended to diminish Samsø’s achievements but to provide perspective on the scale of a national transition to renewable energy.

One crucial conclusion is clear: if Denmark aims to make a similar transition, substantial energy conservation efforts are necessary to reduce overall energy consumption. Samsø’s experience demonstrates that these savings will not happen automatically—it will require substantial effort, supported by economic incentives and penalties. Importantly, every kilowatt-hour saved is more valuable than one produced—it requires no maintenance or service.

A direct application of Samsø’s results to Denmark indicates that transitioning to a renewable energy system nationwide would require an investment of approximately 670 billion DKK (89 billion EUR). However, this transition could yield annual savings of around 60 billion DKK (8 billion EUR), allowing the investment to pay off in just 11 years—a promising return, given the gravity of the challenge and the potential benefits.

30. ID: 958
   Year: 2017
   Cluster: (None)
   Actor: Márcia Filipa Oliveira Alves
   Statement: I see the implementation of renewable energy sources (RES) in isolated power systems, such as islands, as both a challenge and an opportunity. In my view, the intermittency of RES creates issues with grid stability and mismatches between power demand and supply. Each power system, in my opinion, must be carefully designed to make the best use of available local resources and create a diverse energy mix. I believe studies like this one are crucial to overcoming these obstacles by improving our understanding of power systems and available RES. They also allow us to create scenarios that maximize RES penetration while evaluating the economic impacts.

I think Pico and Faial Islands, which are close to each other and have similar electricity demands of about 40 GWh/year, are highly dependent on imported fossil fuels and currently rely on wind energy as their primary RES. However, Faial Island faces significant curtailment of wind energy due to grid stability limits. From my perspective, the forestry biomass potential of these islands is substantial enough to meet total demand, but I caution against shifting dependence from fossil fuels to biomass alone.

I propose a scenario that incorporates a mix of wind energy, biomass, urban waste energy, wave energy, hydro power, and solar PV, along with sodium-sulfur batteries as energy storage systems (ESS). Fossil fuel-fired power plants should still play a role in grid stabilization and power backup. My objective is to increase RES penetration in Pico and Faial Islands, reduce fossil fuel dependence, and address urban waste management challenges. The proposed scenario achieves a 12.55 percentage point increase in RES share, raising it to 27.8% and reducing fossil fuel consumption and CO2 emissions by 16.7%. With an optimized ESS capacity of 8 MWh, the RES share could reach 42.3%, reducing fuel oil consumption and CO2 emissions by 25.5% compared to the 2030 business-as-usual (BaU) scenario.

In my assessment, fossil fuel costs significantly impact the additional annual costs of the system. Depending on fuel prices, the extra annual costs range from 2.5 to 3.5 million euros. Connecting the power systems of the islands would allow for higher RES penetration—up to 42.3%—but comes at a higher annual cost of approximately 2 million euros for an extra 10 percentage points in RES share. Isolated power systems, however, achieve a 32% RES penetration with 57% lower additional costs.

From an environmental perspective, I support connecting the islands’ power systems to maximize RES penetration. Economically, I favor implementing RES in isolated systems due to lower costs. To justify the connection, I believe the islands’ power demand must increase through population growth or industrial development. I also think connecting the islands would enhance social cohesion and should be encouraged.

Finally, I suggest measures that could lead to eliminating fossil fuels entirely in both islands in the long term, serving as a starting point toward a 100% RES energy system for Pico, Faial, and the entire Azores archipelago.
